About

A reading engine, not a chart calculator

Birth data goes in. Words come out — ten chapters on the sectors of life, each written from conditions the text states and this engine tests against the chart.

Why it does not draw the chart

There are many good platforms that will show you a chart. This one exists to answer the question that comes after the chart: what does it mean for me? So the chart is computed and then discarded. What you see is the reading.

Every line carries its verse

Open any statement and you see the Devanagari and the chapter-and-verse it came from. Where a citation could not be checked against a printed edition, the platform says so rather than implying authority it does not have. One fabricated citation is enough to discredit a whole panel — that has already happened here once, and the panel was withdrawn rather than kept with a disclaimer.

Two rules govern the harsh readings

Phaladīpikā 15.3 does not say "a weak lord damages the house". It names a configuration: the lord in an adverse place, combust, in an enemy sign or debilitated, and without benefic association. 15.6 goes further and states that two or three conditions must agree before a severe loss is declared.

So a grave reading that rests on a single placement is withheld, and the engine says that it is withholding it. That is not caution layered over the tradition — it is the tradition's own instruction, which most engines ignore.

No death date

The classical longevity arithmetic is disputed by the tradition itself: 22.24 records that Bhadanta rejected the piṇḍāyu as unsound and that Varāhamihira said the same. The haraṇa reductions are therefore not computed. A figure from a disputed method would be worse than no figure.

What it is built on

Ninety-one verses of Phaladīpikā across chapters 6, 7, 9, 10, 16, 19, 22 and 26, transcribed and checked against the printed 1950 edition. The chart mathematics is a sidereal ephemeris with ṣaḍbala, aṣṭakavarga, vargas and viṃśottarī daśā. The full list is on the sources page.
